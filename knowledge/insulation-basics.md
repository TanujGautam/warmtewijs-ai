---
title: Insulation basics for Dutch homes
source: Milieu Centraal (paraphrased), Warmtewijs method notes
---

## Cavity wall insulation (spouwmuurisolatie)
Most Dutch homes built between roughly 1920 and 1975 have a cavity wall that was left empty. Filling it with beads (EPS parels), glass wool flakes or PUR foam typically costs €20–€30 per m² of wall and is done in one day without breaking anything open. It is often the measure with the shortest payback: 3–6 years. The cavity must be at least 5 cm wide and the outer leaf must be in good condition; an installer checks this with a boroscope camera.

## Roof insulation (dakisolatie)
A pitched roof is insulated from the inside with insulation panels between or against the rafters, costing €40–€80 per m² of roof. Roughly a quarter to a third of heat loss in an uninsulated house goes through the roof. Do it before placing solar panels if the roof is insulated from the outside.

## Floor insulation (vloerisolatie)
With a crawl space (kruipruimte) of at least 35–40 cm, the underside of the floor can be insulated for €25–€45 per m². It makes floors noticeably less cold. Bottom insulation (bodemisolatie, chips on the crawl space floor) is a cheaper alternative for shallow crawl spaces.

## Glazing (HR++ and triple glass)
Replacing single or old double glazing with HR++ costs €150–€250 per m² of glass, including installation. Triple glass insulates better but only pays back when frames are replaced anyway, because existing frames are often too shallow.

## Ventilation
Insulating makes a house more airtight. Without ventilation (trickle vents, mechanical extraction or balanced ventilation) humidity rises and mould can appear. Always pair insulation with a ventilation check.
